Your three-component plan is a good low-budget organic foundation for a fruit garden: 1. Cow dung manure, 2. Bone meal (the broken bones you mentioned, if properly processed and ground), 3. Neem cake (neem khali).

Section 1

What each of your three materials actually does

🐄

1. Cow dung manure

Foundation
  • Adds organic matter.
  • Supplies nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium and other nutrients.
  • Improves soil structure and moisture retention.
  • Supports soil organisms.

This should be the largest component of your plan.

🦴

2. Bone meal

Slow phosphorus source
  • Supplies phosphorus.
  • Typically supplies calcium.
  • Supports root growth and plant establishment.
  • Useful as a slow-release amendment.

Bone meal is not a potassium fertilizer. It does not directly force fruit production.

🌱

3. Neem cake

Nutrient-rich amendment
  • Supplies nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium and organic matter.
  • Improves soil as it decomposes.
  • Can have beneficial effects against certain soil pests, depending on the product and application.

This is a useful addition to your fruit plants.

📊 Approximate nutrient values

Kerala's Farm Guide lists approximate nutrient values of neem cake as 5% N, 1% P₂O₅ and 1.5% K₂O, and bone meal as approximately 3.5% N and 21% P₂O₅. The actual composition varies.

Neem cake · N5%
Neem cake · P₂O₅1%
Neem cake · K₂O1.5%
Bone meal · N3.5%
Bone meal · P₂O₅21%
Bars are scaled relative to the highest value (21%). Source: Kerala Agriculture Department (keralaagriculture.gov.in).

Key insight

Cow dung builds the soil, bone meal adds slow phosphorus and calcium, and neem cake adds balanced nutrients plus soil benefits. Together they are a sensible organic base, but none of them is a strong, quick potassium source.

Section 4

How to mix and apply your three materials

You can mix them together immediately before applying. I wouldn't store a wet mixture for months.

Example: for one medium-sized fruit plant

3 kg mature cow dung compost+ 150 g neem cake+ 75 g bone meal

Procedure

Mix these three materials in a bucket.

Remove weeds around the plant.

Spread the mixture in a circle around the plant, beneath the outer part of its canopy or slightly inside the canopy edge.

Keep the fertilizer away from the main trunk.

Gently mix into the top 2–5 cm of soil if roots aren't exposed.

Water the area.

CautionDo not dig deeply around established fruit trees because you may cut feeder roots.

Section 5

When to apply in Kerala

Because you are in Kerala, you can generally organize your organic feeding around the rainy seasons.

Before the main monsoon, when soil is moist but heavy rain isn't imminent
First application of compost + neem cake
After the main monsoon, when soil is moist and drainage is good
Second application for established plants
During active growth
Maintain watering, mulch and monitor plant condition
During heavy continuous rain
Avoid applying concentrated amendments immediately before heavy rainfall
TipFor large established plants, splitting the quantity into two applications is generally better than dumping everything in one place.

The one nutrient your mixture may lack: potassium

This is important for your fruiting plants.

🐄

Cow dung

N, P, K, organic matter

🌱

Neem cake

N, P, K

🦴

Bone meal

Mainly phosphorus and calcium

However, the potassium supply from these materials can be relatively modest. For fruit development, potassium is an important nutrient.

If your plants are doing wellIf your plants are growing well, flowering and fruiting, don't rush to buy additional fertilizer.
If fruiting is poorIf you observe poor fruit production despite healthy growth and good sunlight, a soil test is preferable before adding extra nutrients. If potassium is needed and you want to keep the cost low, a potassium source such as potassium sulphate can be considered, but it is not necessary to buy it immediately for every plant.
About wood ashWood ash is another potassium-containing material, but it is highly variable and alkaline. Too much can raise soil pH and cause nutrient problems. I would not mix large quantities of ash into your three-component mixture.

Use mature cow dung as the bulk material. Add measured quantities of neem cake and bone meal according to plant size. Apply around the root zone, away from the trunk, and water.

Strong recommendation on bone mealOne thing I would strongly recommend before you buy large quantities of broken bones: make sure you are buying properly processed, dried and ground bone meal, not raw broken animal bones. Raw bones decompose slowly, attract animals and may cause hygiene problems. Bone meal is much easier and more predictable to use.

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Cow dung is the bulk foundation; add measured neem cake and bone meal by plant size.
  • Apply around the root zone, away from the trunk, then water.
  • Split larger amounts into 2 applications around the Kerala rains.
  • Potassium may be the limiting nutrient; soil test before buying more.
  • Fertilizer supports fruiting but cannot guarantee it.
